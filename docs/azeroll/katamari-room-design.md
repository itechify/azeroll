# Katamari room design: a grammar for Azeroll's indoor stages

How Katamari Damacy (2004 / REROLL) and We Love Katamari lay out their house stages, reduced to
rules we can author against in `lions-pride.toml`. This note deliberately does not repeat
[level-design-research.md](level-design-research.md) (density, pacing, hazards),
[katamari-reference.md](katamari-reference.md) (mechanics) or
[campaign-research.md](campaign-research.md) (stage ladder, hubs, how areas open). Read those for the
numbers; read this for *how a room is composed*. Evidence tags as in the other notes:
[C] confirmed on the page, [S] search snippet only, [F] a different Katamari game, [I] inference.

The user's verdict on L1 (2026-11-03) is the brief: "lacking character with everything just sprawled out
everywhere"; "unable to get to certain surfaces like the fireplace … since there are no ramps"; "more of the
rooms should have items in them … fitting thematically to the story"; "there really shouldn't be any
invisible walls INSIDE the inn"; "more character and more intention in the design and layout".

## 1. How the house stages are actually built

All of Katamari Damacy's indoor stages happen in one house, the Takeda Residence
([Katamaripedia](https://katamaripedia.miraheze.org/wiki/Takeda_Residence)) [C]. The same rooms are
re-used at five sizes; what changes is where you start, which doors are open and what is edible.

**The house.** A tatami main room (washitsu) with a kotatsu table in the middle, sliding doors for every
wall, a closet and an alcove with a hanging scroll and the family sword, and a small porch onto the
backyard. A kids' room to the right with stereo equipment and a tiered Hinamatsuri doll stand, where Taro
drives a remote-control car. A kitchen with a large table, a ventilation fan, a gas stove with a fish
broiler, and a second door left ajar to the outside. A front hallway with a lowered genkan and a shoe
cupboard. A bathroom at the end of the hall with a washstand and a squat toilet. A walled backyard with
a vegetable patch (tomatoes, eggplants, cucumbers), two golf holes, a laundry deck, the father golfing, the
dog Butch on the shed, and a two-tiered tiled roof reached from the stone plant row on the right side of
the house [C]. Ants, frogs, snails, mice and sparrows live inside and out; spiders in the crawlspace; cats
roam the house in Make a Star 4 [C]. Every room has one occupation and the props that go with it.

**Make a Star 1 (5 → 10 cm, 3:00 PS2 / 4:00 REROLL, Super 15 cm).** Living room only, "various small
objects all over the table and floor" ([MaS1](https://katamaripedia.miraheze.org/wiki/Katamari_Damacy/Make_a_Star_1)) [C].
You start beside the kotatsu, under which Sleepy Momma is always asleep; what lies on the kotatsu
"varies with her activity" [C]. The first play uses 30 of the room's 145 item kinds, and about a dozen are
offered at once: eyedrops, screws, pushpins, a crayon, cookies, buttons, lipstick, mandarin pieces,
cherries, compasses, hairpins [C]. The replay adds mosquitoes, soy-sauce packs, stamps, caramel boxes,
pencils, erasers, gold coins, matches, chocolates [C]. Cousin Marcy (6.8 cm) runs about *under* the
kotatsu; the guide says to "enter the kotatsu's underside through the floor opposite the sleeping woman"
([KeenGamer cousins](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/guides/katamari-damacy-reroll-cousins-guide)) [C].
Defining vignettes: the kotatsu tabletop (a snack laid out: mandarins, cookies, tea things), the sewing /
stationery spill on the floor beside it, the hidden room *under* the table, and a sleeping person as the
room's immovable landmark.

**Make a Star 2 (5 → 20 cm, 6:00 / 7:00, Super 35 cm).** Living room and the park / backyard
([MaS2](https://katamaripedia.miraheze.org/wiki/Katamari_Damacy/Make_a_Star_2)) [C]. Cousin Havana
"runs around the living room floor near the kotatsu, with some mice and rats", "rolling one of the
oranges" [C]. The backyard can "usually" be entered at 10 cm [C]; the park's far right, "near the water
guns", has 10 cm fences that lead to secret tunnels with shortcuts [C]. The Royal Present: KeenGamer puts
it on Santa's flying eraser-sleigh over the backyard, pulled by fried-chicken-and-magnet reindeer [C];
a TrueAchievements snippet instead puts a scarf on the kitchen table, "around the corner past the 20 cm
barrier", 30 cm to climb the table and 50 cm to take it [S]. Unresolved, but both are the same lesson: a
present is either a lure that moves or a reward on a surface you must climb. Defining vignettes: mice and
a cousin chasing oranges on the floor (a *moving* line of food), sweets near the kotatsu (candy, milk candy,
chocolate, gum first appear here, [StrategyWiki objects](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Katamari_Damacy/Objects)) [S],
a size-gated fence that opens a shortcut, and a garden visible through the porch before you can reach it.

**Make a Star 4 (5 cm → 1 m, 10:00).** The whole house. At 20 cm "the sliding doors in the starting room
open to the rest of the Takeda house" ([KeenGamer presents](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/guides/katamari-damacy-reroll-royal-present-guide)) [C].
The present sits "on the kitchen table, right on the edge facing the Kid's Room"
([MaS4](https://katamaripedia.miraheze.org/wiki/Katamari_Damacy/Make_a_Star_4)) [C]; the route is
"enter the kid's room next to the kitchen, roll onto the sofa, and use it as a ramp to get onto the kitchen
counter", then counter → table [C]. Johnson plays pachinko on the roof with "fancy fish statues", needs
76.9 cm, and "garden plants block the way" until about 1 m [C]. A cobweb-covered crawlspace under the
house links the kitchen, bathroom and front yard [C]. Defining vignettes: a sofa as the ramp to a counter,
a counter as the step to a table, the present on the table *edge* so it is seen from the floor, a roof
party for the end-game, a plant row that is a wall and then food, a dark crawlspace for the small.

**Make Cygnus / Make Cancer (constellation variants of the same house).** Cygnus has "a small attic-like
space with ladders" onto the lower roof and the beams above the porch [C]; its present sits on a cuckoo
clock "high in the living room", reached "by rolling down the ramp from the start area or climbing the
ladder" [C]; cousin Odeko hangs on a hook in the porch rafters, reached by rolling "a sofa to use as a
ramp from the porch onto a sliding door, which works as a makeshift elevator" [C]. Cancer's cousin is in
the doghouse: "get her early. Once you've picked up too much, the doghouse is hard to enter" [C].

**We Love Katamari (2005 / REROLL+).** As Large As Possible 1 (5 → 15 cm, 3:00, Super 30 cm) is a single
child's room: a couch, a dresser with "the ramp to the dresser", and five paper airplanes circling the room
carrying the present, which you catch by waiting under the ramp or by growing until you can take them
over the couch ([GamePretty](https://gamepretty.com/we-love-katamari-reroll-royal-reverie-royal-presents-guide/)) [C].
Cleaning's present sits "on the windowsill right behind the starting point", too big for now, "come back
for it" [C]. The School's present is in a toilet stall "with an unmoving door", so you grow until you can
roll up the stall walls [C]; the Money stage hides one behind boxes under a canopy you cannot fit under if
you get too big [C]; the Exact-Size stage's small present is "on the porch, inside the dog bowl. Grab it
before the dog eats it" [C]. Make a Star 5's tight spaces "you can't fit into once you get bigger" are why
players "reach the 45 cm gate quickly and then clear the lower area" (Steam thread) [S].

**How it was made.** Takahashi "carefully considered the map design and object placement to work
together", then placed by "trial-and-error": add objects, roll them up, adjust
([postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-the-singular-design-of-namco-s-katamari-damacy-2004-)) [C].
Where big objects were missing they scaled small ones and wrote "giant" in front [C]. Long items (pencils)
make the ball arc and waste time [C]. A single-object goal was rejected because it "could overshadow
growing" [C]. The camera zooms out when the ball fills the screen, and he wished it sold scale better [C].
"If it were empty, I would feel empty or lonely" (GDC 2009,
[Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gdc-keita-takahashi---the-complete-gdc-lecture)) [C].
Reviewers' indoor failure mode: "wedged between the floor and a table, and there isn't much you can do
about it", and corners "spew your precious junk" ([search summaries](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=371128)) [S].

## 2. The grammar

Each principle: the rule, why, and the Azeroll pattern.

**P1 Vignettes, not carpets.** Every item group is a small scene with a reason: a snack on a table, a
toolbox tipped over, mice chasing oranges. *Why:* the house reads as a home, and a group's reason tells
you its size (plates on a table are bigger than crumbs on the floor). *Pattern:* one `[[scenes]]` per
vignette, named for the story beat, three to five `items` of different arrangements and mixed models.
Never one model in one arrangement filling a room.

**P2 The nursery is dense, flat and walled by the next tier.** MaS1 is one table and the floor around it,
packed; its edges are things 2× too big. *Why:* the first 30 s teach pickup and steering with no travel
and no failure; big things at the edge are the promise. *Pattern:* a `cluster` + `spill` + `pile` of
0.3–0.5× items within 3 yd of `start`, ringed by 1.5–2× items (`ring`, large spacing) that become edible at
the first tier.

**P3 A line pulls.** Mice in a row, oranges rolling, a crumb trail, shogi pieces: food in a line leading
out of a cluster. *Why:* the eye follows a line; the player never needs a marker. *Pattern:* a `spill`
with a `heading` toward the next vignette and `bend ≤ 0.08`, items sized for the moment you leave; start
each spill inside the previous cluster's edge.

**P4 Bands overlap, and the next target is in frame.** On the kitchen table the present sits on the edge
facing the kid's room; a cousin runs under the table you start beside. *Why:* growth must always have a
visible "soon". *Pattern:* every scene mixes three size classes (now / next / later) with the "later" item
being a single landmark (a keg, a chair, a bed), not a carpet. Tier `at` values follow the landmarks.

**P5 Elevation is built from items.** Sofa → counter → table; "the ramp to the dresser"; a ladder to the
attic; fence ramps outdoors. *Why:* surfaces are reward rooms you earn by routing, and a ramp made of
objects is itself food later. *Pattern:* a `heap` of books/crates/planks with `step` rising to a tabletop,
hearth or bar counter; D29's walkable tops and `ramp`/`stairs` arrangement (pending from the physics agent)
make the climb real. Put the best vignette on the surface so the climb pays.

**P6 Gates are objects or gaps, never invisible.** 10 cm fences, sliding doors that open at 20 cm, a
doghouse you only fit into small, garden plants that block until 1 m, a stall whose walls you roll up.
*Why:* the player sees why they cannot pass and what will change that. *Pattern:* (a) a *small-only*
passage: a real gap under or between furniture (claimed D22 chairs/tables with their legs); (b) a
*big-only* passage: a `solid = true` prop or a claimed keg/crate row that blocks until edible; (c) the only
containment the player cannot beat is a giant visible object at the exit (P7). No `[[barriers]]` indoors.

**P7 The exit is barred by something absurd and obvious.** WLK's canopy, the stall door, the plants.
*Why:* an invisible leash reads as a bug; a toppled keg stack with a "CLOSED" sign reads as a joke and a
promise. *Pattern:* one scaled-up non-collectible or far-oversize model wedged in the doorway (the
postmortem's "giant" trick), lit and visible from the start. Keep the outer `bounds` only as the emergency
backstop.

**P8 Story by placement.** Sleepy Momma under the kotatsu, the father golfing, the RC boy, the broiler
with fish, the shoes in the genkan. *Why:* a room with an occupation is funny and legible; it also seeds
item sizes (the cook's room has pots, the drunk's corner has mugs). *Pattern:* each room gets one
sentence of "what happened here last night" and its props are the evidence; the cast (NPCs, critters) are
the witnesses, placed in the vignette they belong to.

**P9 One landmark per room.** The kotatsu, the doll stand, the broiler, the roof fish statues, the cuckoo
clock. *Why:* orientation without a map, and each landmark is a size milestone. *Pattern:* a single large
claimed fixture or a 1.5–2.5× prop at the room's focal point; tier names can name it.

**P10 Critters are moving food and moving lines.** Mice with Havana, ants, frogs, cats that bolt or bother.
*Why:* motion draws the eye and makes the small tier lively. *Pattern:* a `[[herds]]` entry (`skittish`)
inside the vignette whose story they belong to (rats in the cellar cheese), `fit` sized to the tier.

**P11 Reward shelves must be routable at the size that wants them.** Takahashi's lesson in reverse: a
present on a windowsill you must "come back for" is fine *if* the return path exists. *Why:* "unable to get
to certain surfaces" is the user's second complaint. *Pattern:* every surface with food has a ramp
reachable at the radius its food needs, and nothing on it needs a bigger ball than the ramp admits.

**P12 Rooms open as you grow, and the small rooms come first.** Doors at 20 cm, the backyard at 10 cm,
tight spaces you clear early. *Why:* the stage is a one-way funnel from small rooms to big ones; what you
could not reach becomes edible rather than lost. *Pattern:* route cellar/kitchen pockets at the small
tiers, the common room and landing at the large ones; D10 clearance must hold at every size that can
enter a pocket, and every pocket's content must not grow the ball past its exit.

**P13 Mix tiny dense and big sparse, never uniform.** Density follows size: tabletop crowded, floor open,
one bed. *Why:* uniform spacing is the "sprawl" the user saw. *Pattern:* `cluster` with small `gap` for
the tiny, `line`/`ring` with large spacing for the big; empty floor between vignettes is good if a line
crosses it.

**P14 Humour is literal.** Reindeer made of fried chicken; a present in the dog bowl; a sleeping mother
you cannot wake. *Why:* the joke is in what the object is and where it is, not in text. *Pattern:*
Warcraft objects in wrong jobs (a Stormwind painting as a tray, a boar head wearing a mug), NPC captions
one line long, tier names that name the joke.

## 3. Patterns in recipe vocabulary

| pattern | recipe |
|---|---|
| Nursery | scene at `start`; `cluster` (gap 0.3–0.45, scale ≤ 0.5), `spill` out of it, `ring` of 2× items at radius 3–4 |
| Line that pulls | `spill`, `heading`, `bend 0.04–0.08`, `spacing 0.4–0.6`, count 10–18, mixed `models`, `pick = "cycle"` |
| Meal on a surface | `table-set` on a `table` model with `surface`, or `lift` onto a claimed D22 table top |
| Ramp of things | `heap` with `step` and `heading` (today); D29 `ramp`/`stairs` arrangement when it lands |
| Small-only gap | claimed chairs/benches (legs yield only as the ball nears their gap, D22), a `grid` of crates with a one-ball aisle |
| Big-only blocker | `solid = true` prop row (`line`), or claimed kegs/crates across a doorway |
| Barred exit | one oversize `solid` prop at the vestibule, plus a sign/NPC; no indoor `[[barriers]]` |
| Critters | `[[herds]]` `species`, `model`/`display`, `count`, `at`, `radius`, `fit`, `behaviour = "skittish"` |
| Story witness | cast TOML `[[npc]]` with `quirk`, `carry`, `activity`, placed in its vignette |
| Landmark | a single claimed fixture (bed, canopy bed, bar kegs) or a 1.5–2.5× prop; name the tier after it |
| Overlapping bands | per scene: 60 % now, 30 % next tier, 10 % later; `jitter ≤ 0.1` so sizes stay readable |

## 4. What to avoid

- **Sprawl.** "Everything just sprawled out everywhere": scenes with 3 yd spacing and single-model
  clusters. Fix with P1, P3 and P13: tight vignettes, lines between them, open floor only where a line
  crosses it.
- **Unreachable surfaces.** "Unable to get to certain surfaces like the fireplace where you put some
  items since there are no ramps." Every surface with food gets a ramp (P5, P11) or the food moves to the
  floor.
- **Empty rooms.** "More of the rooms should have items in them … rats and a lot of cheese in the
  basement." Each room is a vignette with an occupation (P8); the cellar and upstairs are routed at the
  sizes that fit them (P12), not sealed.
- **Invisible walls indoors.** "There really shouldn't be any invisible walls INSIDE the inn … it could
  just be a very large item blocking the door." Only P7's barred exit; remove the stair-foot and vestibule
  `[[barriers]]` once the pockets are D10-safe.
- **Uniform carpets.** Apple or mug fields with one spacing; one arrangement per room.
- **Dead ends that trap.** A pocket whose contents grow the ball past its exit (the "wedged between floor
  and table" complaint); D10's sweep must pass at the pocket's largest reachable radius.
- **Long thin props on the route.** Pencils made the real katamari arc; our knives and scrolls do the
  same on a tiny ball. Keep them to side vignettes.
- **Text doing the work.** The world tells the joke; HUD words stay few (D20).
- **Ramps that do not arrive** (playtest 2026-11-03: "the books don't lead up properly onto the bar"). A ramp
  is authored against the surveyed surface, not a remembered one: run the placement validator
  (`WOW_AZEROLL_PLACEMENT=1`, D35) and survey with `WOW_AZEROLL_PLACEMENT_GRID` before trusting a coordinate.
  The rules it encodes: the first step stands on the floor at the foot and its riser is within the climb
  rule of the smallest ball meant to use it (`climb`, default the start radius); each step's origin stays
  over the base floor (props snap to the highest surface under them); the last step stands **at least
  0.05 yd over** the surface it serves (just under it, the live ball stalls at the edge) and overlaps its
  edge; every step is **Wall-state** at that radius (a Dislodge-state step is knocked loose: 0.4-scale
  crates at r 0.32 were); nothing solid stands beside the foot (a chair pushed a r 0.2 ball off the first
  book); a narrow step on sloping ground lets the ball slide off it (hay bales, 1.5 yd wide, hold it).
- **Groups that straddle surfaces.** A cluster whose stragglers fall off a table, a spill that climbs a
  step-rim, a ring that reaches the rail: keep groups inside the surface they decorate (`table-set` with
  `level = true` on table tops, lines along a counter) and declare a non-floor surface with `on`.

## 5. The inn as a Katamari stage: "Last Call at the Lion's Pride" (L1 design, 2026-11-03)

Generated by `docs/azeroll/data/lions-pride-gen.py`; the recipe is `crates/benilla-app/src/azeroll/lions-pride.toml`
(scene comments carry the same room notes), the cast `lions-pride-npcs.toml`. Geometry below comes from the

**The story.** The Stormwind regiment's send-off was last night. The captain's table in front of the fire
got the feast, the bar got the last round, the card game in the west nook ended with a skull on the table,
the cook fled the washing-up, the guests upstairs slept through a pillow fight, and the cellar belongs to the
rats, who have been dragging cheese down there for years. Farley has been polishing the same mug since
dawn and wants his floor back. The front door is blocked by the keg the brewer's cart could not get through it.

**Route and gates** (P6, P12). Nothing is invisible. The pit's 0.62 rim is the first gate: the nursery
must bring the ball to r ≥ 0.25 before the step-rims admit it, and the two supper spills point at the
west and east rims. The bar top, the captain's table, the hearth counter and the kitchen counter are
reward surfaces (P5, P11) each with its own ramp of objects. The kitchen door (2.63) and the cellar
stairwell (3.4) are real gaps that stay passable up to the goal (r 1.2 needs 2.04). The main staircase
(0.25 risers) is open to any ball that can roll; upstairs is the expert detour. The only containment is
the giant keg in the inner vestibule door (`blocker = true`, scene "The door"), seen from the north hall.
The pit and the bar hold about twice the goal's volume on their own, so the basic win is a one-room
Katamari stage (Make a Star 1); the kitchen, cellar, hall and upstairs are the expert content behind the
size grades and the 3 yd challenge, and the goal cannot go above 2.4 without making the 2.63 yd kitchen
door a trap for a ball that grows inside.

### 6.1 The town stages, and what they add to the grammar

Katamari's outdoor stages (Make a Star 4–7 in Katamari Damacy, the town in We Love Katamari) are the house
grammar at street scale, plus four things the house does not need:

**T1 A street is a line.** The town stages are built along roads: you leave the house by the gate, the road
runs past the shops, the park and the school, and the big things (the bus stop, the truck, the giant daikon)
stand at the corners. *Pattern:* one spine (the village street from the inn to the south signpost) carrying
the longest line of goods (P3), with every stall opening onto it.

**T2 Districts are occupations, and each has one size.** The shopping street is pencils-to-bicycles, the
school is children-to-desks, the construction site is cones-to-trucks. *Why:* a district's size tells you
when to come back. *Pattern:* one `[[areas]]` per district, its content banded two tiers wide, and its
landmark (P9) sized for the next district.

**T3 Crowds are moving food and witnesses.** Towns have queues, picnics, people fishing, a bride, a band.
*Why:* a crowd that reacts (D18's escalation) is the town's expression. *Pattern:* the cast sits in the
vignette it belongs to (the judges at the judges' table, the apple-bobbing queue of children, the
auctioneer on his podium), never scattered for coverage.

**T4 Gates are parked things.** The town's size gates are a truck across the road, a fence, a river, the
school gate. *Why:* outdoors there are no doors, so a visible, obviously immovable object is the only honest
wall. *Pattern:* D29 `blocker` props with a reason to be where they are (a hay wagon parked under two
trees, a tent pegged in a gap, tree stumps), never `[[barriers]]`. A gap narrower than the ball is a gate
by itself and needs nothing.

II 8.2, II-b 11.3, III 4.4, IV 8.6, V 3.1), r 3.6 alone and about r 3.9 with the city's own barrels and hay
the autopilot takes on the way; the probe's bridge limit is r 4.0 (r 4.5 wedges). A human who also eats
every claimed barrel in the Trade District could reach r 4.5 and fail the 7 yd bridge: the known hazard of
the funnel, and the reason the Trade District's authored food is small and its stalls are solid.

The Square's ladder is built by `front()` from r 3.5 on arrival, each row's count being what opens the next
step with a 1.08 radius reserve, over row slots read off the placement validator's collision grid (the lane
south of the east strip's raised planter, the nursery and the open middle south of it, the south field, the
south flat on both sides of its kerb, the north flat and field, the east field beside the orphanage and the
lane along the planters' north side; never the stair, the apron or the terrace walks, where a ball sent up
wedged); each row is inset by its piece's quarter-box and a slot narrower than that is skipped. Trees gave 26,000 r³ each at full size; the city's own things give 44 (a gold pile), 117 (the
fountain), 136 (a lion), 741 (Khadgar), so the ladder is the tithe (gold piles pack like the old tree rows
did) with statuary, lions and fountains as each band's landmarks, and it stops at D 76 (r³ 55,000) because
the Square's ground is used up; the finale is D 128 (r³ 262,000) instead of the first pass's 160, with the
pieces at half credit (the bank excluded) and the oversize statuary (the Square's corners D 71–118, the
canal plazas D 107–112, the Trade District D 115–123) carrying the rest at ×1.19 in the thinnest band (D 110)
and ×1.38 at the finale. The displayed finale is 595 yd either way.

### 7.5 Pacing target and result

The 30:00 clock is the human's; par 15:00. The target was an autopilot win in about 10–12 minutes
(≤ 40 % of the clock). The autopilot wins in 8–9½ minutes (run table in ARCHITECTURE.md, "L3 — Stormwind or
Bust!"): about 6½ minutes to the fair gate at D 9 and two minutes for the Square, which it eats from the small
end at 12–18 yd/s. A human who reads the lines, visits the lots, the bank and the orphans' plot and loses the
odd minute to a wall is slower by a third or more, which puts play at the target; the gap is in time per act,
not distance (the autopilot's biggest fixed cost, 50–70 s, is the walk from the convoy to the inner street,
which the Valley watch and the gate passage's hay now feed). Pacing levers that remain: the Square's rows
are eaten in seconds whatever their count; only new ground (the terrace walks, the orphanage block) or more
acts before the Square would add minutes.
